Strong, mobile, athletic.

A performance and longevity programme built for an experienced trainee: strength, rotational athleticism, mobility and recovery held in balance — designed to be used in the gym at 6am and finished feeling capable of more.

Training philosophy

Why the programme is built this way

Do not chase exhaustion. Chase high-quality performance.

The goal is to finish most sessions feeling trained, not destroyed. Every week balances strength and athletic output against genuine recovery, so training stays consistent for years rather than weeks.

Progression & deload

4-week wave
Week 1
RPE 7

Establish baseline working loads.

Week 2
RPE 7–8

Increase load slightly or add reps where appropriate.

Week 3
RPE 8

Hardest week. Avoid unnecessary grinding or repeated failure.

Week 4
Deload

Cut volume ~40–50%. Lighter loads, easy run, drop hill intervals.

After the deload, begin the next wave with slightly improved baseline loads where appropriate. Add load only when the current target can be completed with good technique and within the intended RPE range — never simply because the calendar says to.

Morning training & recovery

Daily practice

Before training

Hydrate. Consider a light carbohydrate source if helpful. Caffeine is optional, per personal tolerance.

After training

Prioritise a substantial meal with protein and carbohydrate within the next few hours.

Daily recovery priorities
  • Sleep: aim for approximately 7.5–9 hours where possible
  • Avoid taking every set to failure — use RPE to regulate effort
  • Adjust volume if sleep, recovery or performance is clearly declining
  • Keep Wednesday genuinely restorative
  • Protect Sunday as a low-stimulation recovery day
Final 5 minutes of gym sessions: a deliberate downshift — gentle stretching, slow breathing, a longer exhale than inhale. Avoid rushing straight from maximal intensity into the rest of the day where possible.

Eating principles

Behind each day's lunch

Aim for approximately 25–35g or more of protein per lunch, depending on total daily requirements.

Adequate protein
Carbohydrates to support training and recovery
Colourful fruit and vegetables
Healthy fats
Foods that are personally well tolerated

If raw vegetables cause bloating, prefer cooked vegetables. If fermented dairy is tolerated, Greek yoghurt or kefir can be an additional option. Batch-cook where practical to make weekday lunches easy.

Not maximum exhaustion — maximum useful adaptation.

Train hard when appropriate. Move well. Keep mobility. Run because you enjoy it. Use yoga to restore movement quality. Protect the nervous system reset day. Finish most weeks feeling capable of doing more — not desperate for a break.
